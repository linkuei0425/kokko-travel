Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

The Czech Republic is part of the EU Schengen Area. Travelers holding a Taiwanese passport (containing a National ID number) enjoy visa-free entry to the Schengen Area for up to 90 days within any 180-day period. Your passport must remain valid for at least 3 months beyond your intended departure date, though having at least 6 months of validity is recommended.

🛃 Customs Declarations

When entering the Czech Republic from a non-EU country, you must declare cash amounts exceeding €10,000 (or the equivalent in foreign currency) to customs. Uninspected meat, dairy products, and controlled substances are strictly prohibited. Personal duty-free allowances include 200 cigarettes (or 50 cigars), 1 liter of spirits with an alcohol content over 22% ABV, or 4 liters of wine.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

For Taiwanese passport holders arriving at Václav Havel Airport Prague (PRG) or entering overland, there is currently no requirement to fill out a physical arrival card. Additionally, the Czech Republic does not have a dedicated online electronic entry declaration system (the EU's ETIAS system is not yet operational, so no advance online registration is needed prior to its launch). Simply present your valid Taiwanese passport, return flight proof, hotel reservation, and travel insurance documentation to the border official upon arrival.

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Czech Koruna (CZK, currency symbol: Kč)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

1. eXchange (Address: Kaprova 14/13, 110 00 Josefov): Widely recognized as the best currency exchange office in Prague, offering top rates with 0% commission. Located near the iconic Old Town Square, simply take Metro Line A to 'Staroměstská' station; it is just a 1-minute walk from the exit. 2. Alfa Prague (Address: Náměstí Republiky 1): Situated right next to the Palladium shopping center, near the famous Powder Tower. Take Metro Line B directly to 'Náměstí Republiky' station. They also provide transparent, competitive exchange rates with zero commission.

💳 Payment Tips

Never exchange money with strangers on the street—this is a notorious scam where you will end up with worthless, out-of-circulation banknotes or foreign currency. While some exchange booths in the Old Town advertise '0% Commission,' their buy rates can be extremely poor. Always check the actual 'We Buy' rate carefully and ask the teller to show you the final amount in Czech Koruna on a calculator before committing. Furthermore, credit cards and Apple Pay are widely accepted throughout Prague, so you only need a small amount of cash for tipping, public restrooms (about 10–20 Kč), or street stalls.
