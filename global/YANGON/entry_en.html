Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Taiwan passport holders must apply for an electronic tourist visa (Tourist eVisa) before traveling to Myanmar. The application costs US$50 and allows a maximum stay of up to 28 days. Once approved, be sure to print out your eVisa Approval Letter and present it at immigration upon arrival.

🛃 Customs Declarations

Taking unauthorized antiques, Buddha statues, or religious artifacts out of the country is strictly prohibited. If you are carrying over US$10,000 in foreign currency cash, it must be declared upon arrival. Narcotics, firearms, weapons, and prohibited publications are completely banned. Furthermore, Myanmar maintains extremely strict regulations on drones—do not bring an aerial drone without a prior official permit, as it risks confiscation or even detention.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

For travelers with Taiwan passports arriving at Yangon International Airport, you are currently still required to fill out a paper Arrival Card and Customs Declaration Form. Flight attendants usually distribute these on the plane, or you can pick them up in the arrival hall. Since Myanmar has not yet rolled out a mandatory online entry declaration system (E-Declaration) for general tourists, be sure to keep a ballpoint pen handy in your carry-on to fill out the paper forms.

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Myanmar Kyat (MMK)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

We highly recommend 'White House Money Exchange' and 'Farmer Money Exchange': 1. White House Money Exchange is conveniently located in the shopping district directly opposite Sule Pagoda in downtown Yangon, near City Hall and the Independence Monument. Simply take a Grab taxi to the Sule Pagoda stop and walk just 2 minutes to reach it. 2. Farmer Money Exchange is situated on the ground floor of a commercial building southwest of Kandawgyi Lake, right near the Parkroyal Hotel. Accessible directly by local bus or taxi, it is well-regarded for its transparent and fair rates.

💳 Payment Tips

Myanmar has extremely strict standards for US dollar bills. Be sure to bring pristine, crisp $100 bills issued in 2013 or later—completely free of creases, stamps, stains, or markings. Slightly folded or older bills are frequently rejected or exchanged at a lower rate. Cash is king in Myanmar; credit cards are generally accepted only at a few large international hotels and upscale restaurants.
