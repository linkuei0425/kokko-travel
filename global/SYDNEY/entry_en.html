Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Taiwan passport holders traveling to Australia for tourism or business must apply for an Electronic Travel Authority (ETA, Subclass 601) in advance. Please download the official "AustralianETA" app on your smartphone to apply online prior to departure. Each stay allows up to 90 days, and the ETA is typically valid for 1 year.

🛃 Customs Declarations

Australia enforces extremely strict biosecurity quarantine regulations. Fresh fruits, vegetables, meat and egg products, plant seeds, soil, and unapproved medicines are strictly prohibited. All traditional Chinese herbal medicines, over-the-counter drugs, and food items must be truthfully declared upon entry. Carrying cash or foreign currency equivalent to AUD 10,000 or more also requires declaration. Travelers aged 16 and above with an ePassport can use the SmartGate automated border processing at Sydney Airport.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

For travelers holding a Taiwan passport, you are currently still required to complete a physical Incoming Passenger Card (IPC) on board your flight to Australia or upon arrival at the airport. Although the Australian government is developing a digital declaration system, the paper IPC remains a mandatory legal document at this stage. It must be truthfully completed in English, signed, and presented alongside your passport at customs.

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Australian Dollar (AUD / A$)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

1. United Currency Exchange (Chinatown Branch): Located right in the heart of Sydney's Chinatown (Shop 7, 207 Thomas St, Haymarket). Getting there: Take Sydney Light Rail line L2 or L3 to the 'Chinatown' stop; it's a quick 3-minute walk from there, conveniently near Paddy's Markets and Market City. 2. Crown Currency Exchange (Town Hall Branch): Situated in the vibrant city center (429 George St). Getting there: Take the Sydney Train to 'Town Hall' station, head out via the QVB (Queen Victoria Building) exit, and walk about 2 minutes. It's close to Sydney Town Hall and the popular QVB shopping area.

💳 Payment Tips

1. Australia is largely cashless! Digital payments are universally accepted—you can simply tap your Visa/Mastercard or use Apple/Google Pay across all public transport (buses, trains, light rail, ferries), restaurants, and shops. You'll only need a small amount of cash for emergencies. 2. Exchange a small amount of cash before your trip, but avoid using currency exchange counters at Sydney Airport, as they charge hefty fees and offer notoriously poor exchange rates. 3. When exchanging cash downtown, opt for reputable, well-established money changers that clearly display their rates and feature a 'No Commission' sign.
