Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Taiwanese passport holders can enjoy visa-free entry to Japan for up to 90 days for tourism, business, or visiting family. Please ensure your passport has at least 6 months of validity remaining.

🛃 Customs Declarations

Strictly prohibited items include meat products (such as jerky, sausages, and canned meats), fresh fruits and vegetables, illegal drugs, and counterfeit goods that infringe on intellectual property rights. Additionally, if you are carrying cash, securities, or gold (weighing 1 kg or more) valued at over 1 million JPY, you must declare it to customs upon arrival.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

Travelers from Taiwan entering Japan can choose between traditional paper entry cards and customs forms (distributed on the flight or available in the arrivals hall) or Japan's official online system, Visit Japan Web (VJW). We highly recommend registering on the VJW website at least 24 hours before departure to fill out your entry details. Save a screenshot of your generated QR code, and you can breeze through immigration with a quick scan—no paper forms required!

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Japanese Yen (JPY / 円)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

1. "Daikokuya Tenjin": Located just a 3-minute walk from Nishitetsu-Fukuoka (Tenjin) Station, right near Iwataya Department Store and Shintenchō Shopping Street. As a renowned Japanese ticket and currency exchange chain, it offers cash exchange with zero hidden fees and rates better than most banks; 2. "World Currency Shop Hakata": Conveniently situated on the 1st floor of the Hakata Asahi Building (near the underground shopping street entrance), just a 1-minute walk from the Chikushi Exit of JR Hakata Station. Operated by the Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group, it offers a prime location along with safe and transparent transactions.

💳 Payment Tips

Credit cards and contactless mobile payments are widely accepted throughout downtown Fukuoka. However, cash is still essential for Fukuoka's famous "Yatai" (nighttime food stalls), traditional shrines, and local eateries. A handy tip: you can easily withdraw Japanese Yen cash directly using an international debit card at any Seven Bank ATM inside 7-Eleven stores across Japan, which offers very reasonable fees and fair exchange rates.
