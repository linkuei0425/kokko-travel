Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Travelers holding a Taiwanese passport (with a National Identification Number) can enjoy visa-free entry to Japan for short-term stays of up to 90 days! Just ensure your passport is valid for at least 6 months before you travel.

🛃 Customs Declarations

Meat products (including processed items like sausages, beef jerky, and meat floss), fresh fruits, vegetables, and uninspected plants or animals are strictly prohibited. Gold products exceeding 1 kg must be declared. Duty-free allowances include up to 3 bottles of alcohol (approx. 760 ml each), 200 cigarettes, 2 ounces of perfume, and other items with a total value under 200,000 JPY.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

Taiwanese passport holders entering Japan can choose one of two declaration methods: 1. [Online Digital Declaration (Highly Recommended)]: Fill out your Immigration and Customs forms on the official 'Visit Japan Web' website before departing to generate a QR Code. Upon arrival, simply scan it at the electronic declaration gates for a fast-track exit! 2. [Paper Entry Cards]: If you didn't register online, you can request paper copies of the Disembarkation Card and Customs Declaration Form from the flight crew or pick them up in the airport arrival hall.

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Japanese Yen (JPY)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

1. Daikokuya Shinjuku Nishiguchi: Conveniently located just a 2-minute walk from the West Exit of JR Shinjuku Station, right near Odakyu and Keio department stores. This iconic brand is one of Japan's premier pawnshop chains and top-rated currency exchange spots. 2. Sakura Exchange Shibuya: Situated only a 3-minute walk from the Hachiko Exit of JR Shibuya Station, close to the world-famous Shibuya Scramble Crossing and SHIBUYA 109. It offers excellent exchange rates and helpful multilingual service.

💳 Payment Tips

1. While digital payments like credit cards, PayPay, and Suica/Pasmo IC cards are now widely accepted throughout Japan, cash remains king at traditional neighborhood shops, ramen vending machines, and festival stalls. It is best to keep 10,000 to 20,000 JPY in cash on hand. 2. You can easily make international cash withdrawals in Yen at 7-Eleven (Seven Bank) or FamilyMart ATMs using your foreign bank debit or credit cards. 3. Ordering your currency online through your local bank before departure and picking it up at the airport usually yields a much better exchange rate than swapping cash after arriving in Japan.
