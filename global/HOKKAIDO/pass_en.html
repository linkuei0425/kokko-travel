Hokkaido Passes FAQ

Q: Can I use nationwide IC cards like Suica, ICOCA, or PASMO in Hokkaido?

Yes! Nationwide IC cards such as Suica, ICOCA, and PASMO, along with the local Kitaca card, are accepted on the Sapporo Municipal Subway, Sapporo Streetcar, major JR Hokkaido rail routes (Sapporo, Otaru, New Chitose Airport), and select Chuo Bus and Hokuto Kotsu routes. However, please keep in mind that unstaffed JR stations in remote parts of Eastern and Northern Hokkaido, as well as local rural buses, still only accept cash.

Q: Are JR trains and airport buses prone to delays during Hokkaido's winter snow season? How should I prepare?

Yes. Winter blizzards and strong winds frequently cause JR train delays or preventive service suspensions, and airport buses can be delayed due to expressway closures. We strongly recommend allowing plenty of extra time for airport transfers and flights, and keeping a close eye on the official 'JR Hokkaido Train Status' website. Unless roads are completely blocked by heavy snow, the JR Rapid Airport train is generally more reliable than highway buses.

Q: Can I use the Sapporo Subway 1-Day Pass (Donichika Pass) for JR trains between New Chitose Airport and Sapporo?

No. The Sapporo Subway 1-Day Pass is strictly valid only on the Sapporo Municipal Subway (Namboku, Tozai, and Toho Lines). It cannot be used on JR rail lines, JR buses, or New Chitose Airport shuttle buses.

Q: Do I need to pay extra for a reserved seat on the JR Rapid Airport train (New Chitose Airport to Sapporo)?

Not necessarily. The Rapid Airport train features both non-reserved cars (standard fare, payable by tapping an IC card) and reserved seat cars called 'u-seat' (which require an additional ¥840 reserved seat ticket). If you are traveling with large luggage or during peak hours, purchasing a 'u-seat' ticket is highly recommended to guarantee a seat and access dedicated luggage racks.
