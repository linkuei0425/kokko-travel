Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Taiwan passport holders enjoy visa-free entry to Malaysia for stays of up to 30 days per visit (non-extendable). Make sure your passport is valid for at least 6 months beyond your entry date, and keep your return or onward flight ticket as well as proof of sufficient funds handy for inspection upon arrival.

🛃 Customs Declarations

Illegal drugs (which carry extremely severe penalties in Malaysia), unlicensed firearms and ammunition, meat products, and endangered species are strictly prohibited. Duty-free allowances permit up to 1 liter of alcohol and 200 cigarettes—anything over must be declared and taxed. You must also file a customs declaration if you are carrying foreign currency or Malaysian Ringgit cash exceeding the equivalent of USD 10,000.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

Paper arrival cards are a thing of the past for Taiwan passport holders! All travelers from Taiwan must complete the online Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) within 3 days prior to arrival (including the day of arrival). Upon completion, the system will generate a PDF confirmation. Simply present your passport alongside your MDAC confirmation—either on your phone or printed out—at immigration.

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Malaysian Ringgit (MYR / RM)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

1. My Money Master: Located at unit LG-040 on the LG Floor of Mid Valley Megamall. How to get there: Take the KTM Komuter train directly to Mid Valley Station. Nearby landmark: Right near the outer entrance of the AEON Supermarket on the LG floor. This is widely known as the physical money changer with the best exchange rates in Kuala Lumpur, so expect a bit of a line! 2. Vital Rate: Located on Level 1 (Unit 1.12.00) in the Pavilion Elite zone of Pavilion KL. How to get there: Take the Monorail to Bukit Bintang Station and walk about 5 minutes. Nearby landmark: Near the main entrance of Pavilion KL, directly opposite the JW Marriott Hotel.

💳 Payment Tips

1. Exchanging TWD to MYR directly in Taiwan gives terribly poor rates. It is highly recommended to bring crisp cash (preferably $1,000 NTD bills) and convert it at licensed local money changers in Kuala Lumpur, where the rates are vastly superior to banks in Taiwan or airports. 2. Currency exchange counters at Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA/KLIA2) offer less favorable rates; exchange only a small amount (such as RM 50–100) to cover transit into the city center. 3. Credit cards are widely accepted at department stores, chain outlets, and for Grab rides throughout downtown Kuala Lumpur, but street food stalls, night markets, and smaller local eateries remain cash-only.
