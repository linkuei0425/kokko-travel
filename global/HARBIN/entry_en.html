Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Entry into Mainland China is straightforward—all you need is a valid Mainland Travel Permit for Taiwan Residents (Taiwan Compatriot Permit), with no extra visa or endorsement required. If you forgot your permit or it has expired, don't worry! You can easily apply for a single-entry permit right upon arrival at the port visa office in Harbin Taiping International Airport.

🛃 Customs Declarations

Strictly refrain from bringing meat products, fresh fruits, vegetables, or uninspected plants, animals, and related items into the country. Additionally, if you are carrying more than RMB 20,000 in cash or foreign currency exceeding equivalent to USD 5,000, you must submit a written declaration to Customs.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

Holders of a Taiwan passport and Taiwan Compatriot Permit do not need to fill out a physical arrival card, as these are meant solely for foreign travelers. Great news—China Customs has officially lifted all health declaration requirements (the 'black code'), so online registration via the WeChat mini-program is no longer needed. However, if you experience respiratory symptoms such as a fever or cough, please proactively report them to Customs officers.

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Chinese Yuan (CNY / RMB)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

For the best currency exchange, head to the Bank of China's Harbin Central Street Branch (Address: No. 110 Central Street, Daoli District, Harbin, Heilongjiang Province). It's just a 300-meter walk from Exit 1 of Central Street Metro Station on Line 2, right next to the iconic Central Street Pedestrian Mall and the historic Modern Hotel. Alternatively, try the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC) branch nearby at No. 106 Central Street. Both major state-owned banks offer transparent, highly competitive exchange rates without charging steep service fees.

💳 Payment Tips

Mobile payments via WeChat Pay and Alipay dominate daily spending in Mainland China, so it's highly recommended to link your credit or debit card to these apps before your trip. Keep physical cash primarily as a backup. When exchanging currency at a bank, always request and safely store your Exchange Memo (receipt), as you'll need it to convert any unused RMB back into your home currency before departing the country.
