Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Taiwanese passport holders are required to obtain a visa to visit Vietnam. You can easily apply for a 90-day single-entry or multiple-entry e-Visa directly through the official Vietnam Immigration website. The fee is US$25 for a single-entry visa and US$50 for a multiple-entry visa. Processing typically takes 3 to 5 business days, so we recommend applying at least two weeks before your departure and bringing a printed paper copy for verification.

🛃 Customs Declarations

When entering Vietnam, the maximum cash limit allowed without customs declaration is US$5,000 (or equivalent in foreign currency) and 15 million VND. Your duty-free allowances include 200 cigarettes, 1.5 liters of spirits (above 22% ABV), and 2.0 liters of alcoholic beverages (under 22% ABV). Please note that narcotics, weapons, unauthorized publications, and illegal drugs are strictly prohibited.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

Good news for Taiwanese passport holders arriving on international flights at Hanoi's Noi Bai International Airport (HAN): you do not need to fill out a paper arrival card! Additionally, Vietnam currently has no mandatory online entry declaration system (all COVID-19 health declarations have been fully phased out). Simply present your original passport (valid for at least 6 months) along with a printed copy of your e-Visa to head straight through immigration control.

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Vietnamese Dong (VND)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

Ha Trung Street (Phố Hà Trung) in Hanoi's Old Quarter is famous for offering the best currency exchange rates in town, with "Quốc Trinh Ha Trung" (Address: 27 Phố Hà Trung, Hàng Bông, Hoàn Kiếm, Hà Nội) being the top recommended spot. How to get there: Located conveniently in the Old Quarter, it's just a leisurely 5 to 8-minute walk from the iconic St. Joseph's Cathedral, or a quick 3 to 5-minute Grab ride from the west side of Hoan Kiem Lake.

💳 Payment Tips

1. Bring crisp, clean $100 USD bills (2013 series or newer, with no creases or damage) for the exchange, as they fetch significantly better rates than smaller bills or other currencies. 2. Always count your cash on the spot! Vietnamese Dong has many zeros and certain notes share similar colors (like 500,000 and 50,000 VND), so double-check carefully. 3. Airport exchange booths offer poor rates, so it's best to convert just enough cash there to cover your cab ride into the city.
