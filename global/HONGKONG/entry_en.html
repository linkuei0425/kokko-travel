Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Good news for Taiwan passport holders! You can enjoy a visa-free stay in Hong Kong for up to 30 days. For travelers from other nations, a visa might be required, so it's always best to check the official Hong Kong Immigration Department website to ensure a smooth entry.

🛃 Customs Declarations

When passing through customs, remember that drugs, firearms, explosives, unprocessed endangered species, and pirated or obscene materials are strictly prohibited. For tobacco and alcohol, there are duty-free allowances: you can bring in 19 cigarettes or 25 grams of cigars/tobacco, and 1 liter of alcoholic beverages over 30% alcohol by volume. If you exceed these limits, be sure to declare them and pay the necessary duties to avoid any hassle.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

Entering Hong Kong is a breeze – no need to fill out an arrival card! Just have your valid passport and visa (if required) ready, and you can swiftly pass through immigration at either the convenient self-service e-channels or traditional manned counters. A quick heads-up: if you're carrying more than HKD 120,000 or its equivalent in foreign currency, remember to declare it to customs upon arrival.

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Hong Kong Dollar (HKD)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

While it's wise to exchange a small amount of Hong Kong Dollars at a bank in Taiwan before your trip for immediate needs, upon arrival in Hong Kong, you'll find various options. Airport exchange counters are convenient but typically offer less favorable rates. For better exchange rates, head to city exchange shops like those found in Chungking Mansions or Banyan Exchange, or visit local banks. However, for ultimate convenience, using an Octopus card or credit card for payments is often your best bet.

💳 Payment Tips

Good news for travelers: most shops and restaurants across Hong Kong are happy to accept credit cards and Octopus card payments, making transactions a breeze. However, for those charming small purchases, specific mini-bus routes, or exploring vibrant local wet markets, a bit of cash will come in handy. We recommend carrying a small amount of physical currency for these occasions, reserving your credit card for bigger splurges. And here's a savvy traveler's secret: steer clear of exchanging large amounts of Hong Kong Dollars at the airport, as you'll typically find much better rates elsewhere!
