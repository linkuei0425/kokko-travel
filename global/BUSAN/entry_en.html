Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Taiwan passport holders enjoy visa-free entry to South Korea for up to 90 days. The Korean government has temporarily waived the K-ETA (Electronic Travel Authorization) requirement for travelers from 22 countries and regions, including Taiwan (extended through December 31, 2024). This means you can enter smoothly without needing to apply or pay for an advance authorization beforehand!

🛃 Customs Declarations

Please note that all meat products (including pork jerky, sausages, and instant noodles containing meat components), as well as uninspected fresh fruits, vegetables, and agricultural items, are strictly prohibited. The personal duty-free allowance is US$800. Carrying foreign currency exceeding US$10,000 (or equivalent) must be declared to customs. Duty-free limits for alcohol allow up to 2 bottles (totaling up to 2 liters and under US$400 in total value), while cigarettes are limited to 200 sticks.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

Travelers with a Taiwan passport can choose between filling out a paper arrival card or using the convenient online declaration system: 1. [Paper Arrival Card]: If you haven't completed the form online, flight cabin crew will hand out physical Arrival Cards. Each traveler must fill out their accommodation address and contact number in South Korea in either English or Korean, then hand it to the immigration officer. 2. [Online Electronic Declaration]: Korea has introduced an electronic arrival card system, allowing travelers to submit entry details online within 3 days prior to departure. Furthermore, if you have nothing to declare, simply walk through the Green Channel—paper customs declaration forms are no longer needed for those with nothing to declare! You can also complete your health declaration on the Q-CODE website ahead of time to receive a QR code for a faster, seamless clearance process.

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

South Korean Won (KRW / ₩)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

The most highly recommended currency exchange booths offering the best rates in Busan are Woori Exchange and Youngjin Exchange, both located in Nampo-dong. [Directions & Landmarks]: Take Busan Metro Line 1 to Nampo Station and head out Exit 7. Walk for about 3 minutes down an alley along the Gwangbok-ro Shopping Street near the entrance to Yongdusan Park. The two shops sit right next to each other, making it super easy to compare rates on the spot!

💳 Payment Tips

1. It is best to exchange a small amount of KRW before your trip (or withdraw some at a Gimhae Airport ATM) to cover your initial transportation costs. Then, bring crisp, large-denomination cash (such as $100 USD or 1,000 TWD bills) to Nampo-dong for the rest of your exchange—the rates here are far better than at domestic banks or Korean airports. 2. Exchange booths generally only accept undamaged, large-denomination bills (e.g., $100 USD or 1,000 TWD). 3. Alternatively, you can take advantage of a WOWPASS or NAMANE card to exchange foreign cash and top up directly at self-service kiosks located throughout Busan metro stations and airports.
