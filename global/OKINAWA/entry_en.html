Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Travelers holding a Taiwan passport with a National Identification Number can enter Japan visa-free for short-term stays of up to 90 days. Your passport must be valid upon entry, though keeping at least 6 months of remaining validity is highly recommended.

🛃 Customs Declarations

All fresh meats and processed meat products (including jerky, sausages, vacuum-sealed meats, and instant noodles containing meat), along with fresh fruits and vegetables, are strictly prohibited. Carrying cash or foreign currency equivalent to over 1 million JPY requires a mandatory customs declaration. The duty-free allowance for alcohol is up to 3 bottles (around 760ml each), and up to 200 cigarettes.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

Travelers heading to Japan can choose between online declaration or traditional paper forms. 1. Online Declaration (Recommended): We strongly advise completing your registration on the official 'Visit Japan Web (VJW)' website before departure to obtain your QR codes for Immigration and Customs. Simply scan them on your phone for a smooth process upon arrival. 2. Paper Forms: If you haven't declared online, you can request paper forms from flight attendants or grab them in the airport arrival hall. Fill out one Immigration Card per person and one Customs Declaration form per family in Traditional Chinese or English to clear customs.

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Japanese Yen (JPY / ¥)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

1. Daikokuya (Naha Kokusai-dori): Perfectly positioned in the heart of Naha's vibrant Kokusai-dori, just a 6-minute walk from Miebashi Monorail Station and right near Don Quijote, offering fantastic exchange rates. 2. Chance Center Currency Exchange (Naha RYUBO): Super convenient and easy to find, located just a 2-minute walk from Kencho-mae (Prefectural Office) Station right by the 1st-floor entrance of RYUBO Department Store (Palette Kumoji).

💳 Payment Tips

While credit cards and digital payments are becoming more common in Okinawa, Japanese Yen cash is still essential for local buses, traditional eateries, local Okinawa noodle shops, and historic street markets. We recommend exchanging cash before your trip or bringing an international debit card to withdraw Yen directly at 7-Eleven stores using a Seven Bank ATM—it's usually much more convenient and offers better rates than exchanging physical foreign cash on the spot!
