Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Taiwanese passport holders can enjoy visa-free tourist entry to South Korea for up to 90 days. Furthermore, from April 2023 through December 31, 2024, the South Korean government has temporarily waived the Electronic Travel Authorization (K-ETA) requirement for visitors from 22 countries and regions, including Taiwan—meaning you don't need to apply for a K-ETA prior to your trip during this period!

🛃 Customs Declarations

[Strictly Prohibited] Meat and meat products (including sausages, beef jerky, instant noodles with meat packets, etc.), fresh fruits, vegetables, plants, and soil. [Duty-Free Allowances] Each adult is entitled to up to $800 USD in duty-free goods. Additional specific duty-free limits include: 200 cigarettes, 2 bottles of alcoholic beverages (totaling under 2 liters and valued under $400 USD), and 100ml of perfume. [Currency Declaration] Carrying cash equivalent to over $10,000 USD requires a declaration to customs upon arrival.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

Taiwanese travelers entering South Korea without a K-ETA must complete a physical Arrival Card on the plane or upon arrival at the airport. For customs declarations, as of 2023, travelers with nothing to declare can simply proceed directly through the Green Channel without filling out a paper form. You can also submit an electronic customs declaration via the official 'Korea Customs Declaration for Tourists' app. Additionally, we highly recommend completing the online 'Q-CODE' health declaration system within 3 days prior to departure. Presenting your generated QR Code at border control will save you valuable time in quarantine queues (if not completed online, you'll need to fill out a paper yellow health declaration form on-site).

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

South Korean Won (KRW / ₩)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

Myeongdong's "Embassy Money Exchange" and "Yi Pin Xiang Money Exchange". [Directions & Landmarks] Take Seoul Subway Line 4 to Myeongdong Station (424). Exit from Exit 5 or 6, walk straight, and turn left at the first alley (right next to ISAAC Toast). Continue straight for about 200 meters, then turn right at the alley running along the outer wall of the former Chinese Embassy. The two exchange booths are only a few meters apart, perfectly situated in the heart of the Myeongdong shopping district, right in front of the main entrance of the former Chinese Embassy.

💳 Payment Tips

1. It is recommended to bring crisp NT$1,000 bills (or US$100 bills) from home and exchange them directly at Myeongdong money changers after arriving in South Korea—the rates here are far better than those at local Taiwanese banks or Korean airport counters. 2. Exchange only a small amount at the airport (around NT$2,000 to NT$3,000) to cover your first day's transportation and emergency expenses. 3. Credit cards and mobile payments are widely accepted across South Korea. Alternatively, you can get a WOWPASS or NAMANE card, top it up directly with TWD at automated kiosks, and use it seamlessly as both a T-money transit card and a debit card for everyday shopping.
