Entry & Visa Requirements

🛂 Visa & Passport

Travelers holding a Taiwan passport enjoy visa-free entry to Japan for up to 90 days for tourism, visiting family, or business. Just make sure your passport is valid for at least 6 months!

🛃 Customs Declarations

Meat products (including sausages, jerky, and meal packs containing meat), fresh fruits and vegetables, animal and plant products, and prohibited drugs are strictly forbidden. If you are carrying more than 1 million JPY in cash (or foreign currency equivalent), you must submit a customs declaration form. Please note that all gold items must be declared, regardless of weight.

📝 Arrival Card / E-Declaration

Taiwan passport holders have two convenient entry declaration options before arriving in Japan: 1. Paper Declaration: Ask your flight crew during the flight or pick up a physical 'Disembarkation Card' and 'Customs Declaration' form in the Kansai International Airport (KIX) arrival hall to fill out by hand. 2. Online Declaration (Highly Recommended!): Use Japan's official 'Visit Japan Web' (VJW) system. Simply register and submit your immigration and customs details on the VJW website before departure to generate a QR Code. Upon arriving at Kansai Airport, scan your QR Code at the electronic declaration gates for a swift, smooth clearance experience that saves you precious travel time!

Currency & Payments

💰 Local Currency

Japanese Yen (JPY / ¥)

💡 Best Exchange Spots

1. Daikokuya Shinsaibashi: Perfectly located in the heart of the Shinsaibashi Shopping Street, just a 3-minute walk from Exit 5 of Shinsaibashi Station on the Osaka Metro Midosuji Line, right near Daimaru Department Store and Chow Tai Fook. 2. Konan Ticket Umeda: Situated on the B1 floor of the Osaka Ekimae No. 2 Building, about a 5- to 8-minute walk via the underground street from JR Osaka Station or Umeda Subway Station. It offers transparent exchange rates that are much better than those at the airport.

💳 Payment Tips

We recommend exchanging local currency for Japanese Yen cash at a bank before your trip, or bringing a debit card with overseas withdrawal functionality to get cash from Seven Bank ATMs across Japan. If you need to exchange money locally in Osaka, discount ticket shops (kinken-ten) like those listed above or specialized currency exchange counters offer far better rates than airports or hotels. Additionally, while credit cards and transit IC cards (such as ICOCA and Suica) are widely accepted in most of Osaka's shopping malls, convenience stores, and chain restaurants, traditional local shops, shrines, and temples still exclusively accept cash.
